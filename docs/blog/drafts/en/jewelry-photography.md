When photographing polished jewelry, shape what the metal reflects. Put diffusion between the light and the piece for a broad highlight, and use a small dark card to define the edge. Review stones, prongs and engraving at full size before moving to the next piece.

## How do you get an even reflection?

Start on a neutral surface. Make the light source larger in relation to the piece with a diffuser, then move it until the reflection outlines the metal. Use a small dark card where two polished edges merge.

Set the tripod and check focus on the nearest stone or fastening. If you change aperture for depth, check fine engraving again before shooting all versions.

## Which details should you reserve a frame for?

Keep a main view, a view of the fastening and a close-up of the setting or marking. Lock colour and framing for variants and compare the files next to each other.

## What should you check before publishing?

| Symptom                         | Adjustment                                              |
| ------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| White glare hides engraving     | Move diffusion or lower exposure slightly               |
| Silver looks like a white shape | Add dark edge reflection                                |
| Setting is blurred              | Adjust focus plane or use an appropriate depth of field |

## FAQ

### Should I use a light tent?

It can simplify reflections. Add a dark card or move part of the diffusion when the tent leaves the piece without a readable edge.

### How should I check gemstone colour?

Keep the light and white balance stable and compare with the real item. Different displays can still show variations.

### What is the best hero angle?

Choose the view that shows the shape and the stone or main feature. Add a second photo for the clasp or back.
