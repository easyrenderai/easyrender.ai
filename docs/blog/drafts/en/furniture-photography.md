Start with a three-quarter photo that shows the front and depth of the furniture. Add a straight front view, a side or back and close-ups of the finish and hardware. Use the same setup across variants so shoppers can tell when the wood, upholstery or construction actually changes.

## How do you light a large piece of furniture?

Put a window or a large diffused light on one side, a little forward of the piece. Turn off room lights if their colour is different. Use a white board opposite the light to lift dark areas without flattening the grain.

Place your camera far enough back to see the whole piece, then adjust its height until the vertical edges look straight. Mark the tripod and furniture position on the floor. Use those marks for each colour or fabric.

## Which photos should you take before moving the setup?

Use the main image to show the silhouette. A straight-on view explains the front, and a side view shows depth and the shape of legs or arms. Photograph doors and drawers open when they change what a buyer can use.

Take the hardware and material close-ups after the full views. Photograph a seam or join as well as the fabric or grain. Put measured dimensions in a labeled diagram or the specifications.

## What should you check before publishing?

| Problem                              | Fix                                            |
| ------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------- |
| The top looks wider than the base    | Move the camera back and keep it level         |
| Oak looks yellow in the side photo   | Turn off mixed lighting and lock white balance |
| The rear appears only in a room shot | Add a full, unobstructed rear view             |

## FAQ

### Can I photograph furniture with a phone?

Yes. Use the main lens in good light and support the phone where you can repeat the angle. Back up before switching to an ultra-wide lens. Check the first picture enlarged for stretched corners and blurred texture.

### Should I show the room around the furniture?

Use a room photo to show a possible setting. Keep a separate product shot where the outline, base and finish are clear. The specification table should carry the measurements.

### Do all variants need the same gallery?

Repeat the main angle when the finish or construction changes. Reuse detail images only when the photographed part is genuinely identical. Label which colour or assembly the image shows.
