Start with a three-quarter photo that shows the front and depth of the furniture. Add a straight front view, a side or back and close-ups of the finish and hardware. Use the same setup across variants so shoppers can tell when the wood, upholstery or construction actually changes.

![Editorial rendering: sideboard, half solid and half wireframe](/blog/illustrations/furniture-photography/hero.webp "Editorial illustration made in Blender.")

## How do you light a large piece of furniture?

Put a window or a large diffused light on one side, a little forward of the piece. Turn off room lights if their colour is different. Use a white board opposite the light to lift the shadows and keep the grain visible, as in [Shopify’s product photography setup](https://www.shopify.com/blog/product-photography).

Place your camera far enough back to see the whole piece, then adjust its height until the vertical edges look straight. Mark the tripod and furniture position on the floor. Use those marks for each colour or fabric.

## Which photos should you take before moving the setup?

Use the main image to show the silhouette. A straight-on view explains the front, and a side view shows depth and the shape of legs or arms. Photograph doors and drawers open when they change what a buyer can use.

Take the hardware and material close-ups after the full views. Photograph a seam or join as well as the fabric or grain. Put measured dimensions in a labeled diagram or the specifications.

## What should you check before publishing?

| Problem                                 | Fix                                                                  |
| --------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| The top looks wider than the base       | Move the camera back and keep it level                               |
| Oak looks yellow in the side photo      | Turn off mixed lighting and lock white balance                       |
| The rear appears only in a room shot    | Add a full, unobstructed rear view                                   |
| Drawer fronts show a white hotspot      | Diffuse the light and change its angle                               |
| A leg blends into the background        | Move the backdrop or add fill from the side                          |
| Two finishes look like the same variant | Repeat the lead shot at the same settings and add material close-ups |

## Build a shot list for one piece

For the table in the reference photo, the three-quarter view shows the top and the structure below it in one frame. Add a straight profile when shoppers need to see the thickness of the top and the shape of its supports. Use a detail to show where the parts meet.

| Shot          | Question to answer   | What to keep in the frame                    |
| ------------- | -------------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| Three-quarter | What shape is it?    | Full top, base and legs                      |
| Side          | How deep is it?      | The thickness of the top and the whole base  |
| Joint detail  | How is it assembled? | The connection between the top and the frame |

## Keep a record of your setup

| Record                            | When to check it                         |
| --------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| Tripod position and lens setting  | Before switching to another piece        |
| White balance and reference frame | After changing the light or location     |
| Product and variant identifier    | Before exporting images to the catalogue |

## FAQ

### Can I photograph furniture with a phone?

Yes, you can photograph furniture with a phone using the main lens in good light and support the phone to repeat the angle. Back up before switching to an ultra-wide lens, then check the first picture enlarged for stretched corners and blurred texture. Use a tripod or stable surface to maintain consistency.

### Should I show the room around the furniture?

Use a room photo to show a possible setting and keep a separate product shot where the outline, base and finish are clear. Use neutral light and decor so the finish matches the plain-background photo. Put verified measurements in the specifications, where the shopper can find them quickly.

### Do all variants need the same gallery?

Repeat the main angle when the finish or construction changes, using the same framing across versions. Reuse a detail only when the photographed part is identical. Label the colour or assembly you show so customers can connect each image to the variant they select.
