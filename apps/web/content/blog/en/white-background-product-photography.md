Set the camera, product and lights in fixed positions so you can repeat the shot. Light the product and the white background separately when possible. Expose for the product first, then check its pale edges, material and contact shadow before you export the image.

![Editorial rendering: lidded bottle, half solid and half wireframe](/blog/illustrations/white-background-product-photography/hero.webp "Editorial illustration made in Blender.")

## How do you set up the white background?

Curve white paper up behind the item so there is no horizon line. Place a large diffused light to one side and use a white reflector to soften the opposite shadow. For a pale item, use a dark card just out of frame to make its edge readable.

Keep the camera, product and lights fixed for your first comparison. Adjust one control at a time. In the final file, zoom in on the lightest product detail and the base.

## How much should you retouch?

Correct colour and dust before adjusting the white point. If you mask, inspect the edge on grey to see halos. Preserve believable contact unless the destination’s image guidelines require a different treatment.

Export a proof at the size the site uses and check it against neighbouring products. Retain an original with the lighting and framing unchanged for repeat orders.

## What should you check before publishing?

| Symptom                | Change                                         |
| ---------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| Pale handle disappears | Use negative fill and check highlight clipping |
| Grey background        | Brighten the backdrop independently            |
| Product floats         | Restore edge detail or a soft contact shadow   |

## Check where the product ends

Use a pale item for the test so you can see whether the exposure preserves its outline. The crop above keeps the product edge and the surface texture visible. Adjust the background separately from the product before changing the whole image.

| On the screen                    | Check first                                 | Change                                   |
| -------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| Grey corners around the object   | Uneven light on the backdrop                | Move the backdrop or reposition the fill |
| Pale edge blends into white      | Light spilling from backdrop onto product   | Separate them and flag stray light       |
| Hard shadow behind object        | Light source appears small from the product | Diffuse it and compare again             |
| White fringe survives retouching | The mask softened too wide an area          | Reduce the brightening around the edge   |
| Texture looks washed out         | Highlights clipped on the product           | Lower the product exposure               |
| Product looks like it floats     | All contact shadow was removed              | Retain or soften the original shadow     |

## FAQ

### Can I work with one light?

Yes, you can work with one light. Use a diffused light or window with a reflector, separate the product from the backdrop, and correct the background locally. Next, check your lighting angle and distance to ensure consistent product placement and shadow control.

### Is pure white always required?

Check the destination’s requirements before setting the background colour. Use white where the channel specifies it. On your own store, also check that the product outline is readable at thumbnail size and that the lighting leaves enough detail to tell light materials apart.

### How do I avoid a white fringe?

Review the mask on a darker canvas at full image size. Undo excess edge brightening, then tune the mask narrowly around the affected area.

Toggle between white and dark backgrounds to find which pixels belong to the object. Zoom in again after resizing the final export.
